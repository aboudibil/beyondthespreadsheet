Bring your policy problem

What would your problem look like as a proof of concept?

Some public problems resist every reasonable fix. Usually the money isn't the issue. The parts are connected. A proof of concept maps those connections and sets out the approach a policy could take, before any model is built.

Does this sound familiar?

Problems a spreadsheet can't see.

  • ◆ Every push fails, until one suddenly works, and no one can say why.
  • ◆ Two reforms each did nothing alone. Together they did a lot.
  • ◆ The same programme, launched in a different order, gave a different result.
  • ◆ The average improved, but the people it was meant for fell further behind.
  • ◆ The effect was large on paper and a fraction of that on the ground.

Patterns like these show up across domains: health, skills, culture, infrastructure, public services. They come from structure, so mapping the structure is the first step to seeing where they come from.

What a proof of concept is

A working document, before the model.

The question
What conventional analysis can't answer about the problem, and why.
The approach to adopt
Where the problem is stuck, the routes out of it, and the condition that binds: which levers must move together for any of them to work.
The preliminary architecture
The model's components (actors and their network, narratives, feedback loops, place, distribution, uncertainty) and how they connect.
Grounded and honest
Every mechanism traces to a named, published source. Assumptions are declared, and the document says what it claims and what it doesn't. It shows that the approach can be applied. It takes no policy position.

The model comes after. It is built from the proof of concept once the document has been checked.

How one is built

Four steps, one decisive check.

1 · Test the fit
The approach fits a problem that has a trap with a possible escape, a coordination condition that binds, and an outcome the averages hide. If those are missing, say so and stop.
2 · Anchor in the literature
Search the published research for how the problem behaves. Every mechanism must land on a named source. An AI assistant can search and draft; it cannot vouch.
3 · Map the structure
Who is involved, the stories that frame the problem, the feedback loops, the trap, the escape routes and the binding condition. Together these form the preliminary architecture.
4 · Write it, then verify it
Every reference must be real and say what it is claimed to say, and every claim must stay within what the sources support. This check decides whether a model is worth building.
