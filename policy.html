What would your policy look like as a proof of concept?

A public policy is more than a fix for a problem. It is how a state pursues goals, expresses priorities and steers change. A proof of concept maps the system a policy acts on and sets out the approach it could take, before any model is built.

Where a policy starts

Not only a problem.

Public policy has been defined simply as whatever governments choose to do or not to do (Dye). Policy analysts also note that policies shape how problems are understood, rather than only responding to them (Bacchi). So a proof of concept can start from any of these:

An ambition
A goal a government wants to pursue: grow a sector, sustain a language, widen access to a service.
A priority
A value a government wants to express and protect over time, including value no market prices.
An intervention
A programme or reform to understand, before launch or after it.
A situation that resists change
Where reasonable efforts keep falling short of what was intended.
Does this sound familiar?

What a spreadsheet can't see.

  • ◆ Every push fails, until one suddenly works, and no one can say why.
  • ◆ Two reforms each did nothing alone. Together they did a lot.
  • ◆ The same programme, launched in a different order, gave a different result.
  • ◆ The average improved, but the people it was meant for fell further behind.
  • ◆ The effect was large on paper and a fraction of that on the ground.

Patterns like these show up across domains (health, skills, culture, infrastructure, public services) and whatever a policy sets out to do. They come from structure, so mapping the structure is the first step to seeing where they come from.

What a proof of concept is

A working document, before the model.

The question
What conventional analysis can't answer about the policy, and why.
The approach to adopt
Where the system could settle, the routes towards what the policy intends, and the condition that binds: which levers must move together for any of them to work.
The preliminary architecture
The model's components (actors and their network, narratives, feedback loops, place, distribution, uncertainty) and how they connect.
Grounded and honest
Every mechanism traces to a named, published source. Assumptions are declared, and the document says what it claims and what it doesn't. It shows that the approach can be applied. It takes no policy position.

The model comes after. It is built from the proof of concept once the document has been checked.

How one is built

Four steps, one decisive check.

1 · Test the fit
The approach fits a policy whose system can settle in more than one state, where a coordination condition binds, and where averages hide part of the outcome. If those are missing, say so and stop.
2 · Anchor in the literature
Search the published research for how the system behaves. Every mechanism must land on a named source. An AI assistant can search and draft; it cannot vouch.
3 · Map the structure
Who is involved, the stories that frame the policy, the feedback loops, where the system could settle, the routes towards the intended outcome and the binding condition. Together these form the preliminary architecture.
4 · Write it, then verify it
Every reference must be real and say what it is claimed to say, and every claim must stay within what the sources support. This check decides whether a model is worth building.
